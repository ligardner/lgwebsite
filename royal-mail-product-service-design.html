Royal Mail / Mobile product / Customer retention

Reframing the critical design challenge to improve customer retention.

The brief was a broad UI refresh. I challenged it and refocused the work on the service journey most likely to influence customer behaviour — a sharper target, a tighter MVP and a faster route to evidence.

What I did, in four moves

  1. 01 ChallengeSituation

    Challenge the predefined solution.

    Royal Mail was tackling a retention problem with a broad UI refresh. I challenged that direction: changing the interface alone would not fix the parts of the experience that drive customer behaviour.

    Shifted the brief from “refresh the UI” to “fix the journey that matters”.

  2. 02 DefineTask

    Define where design can move the needle.

    Using customer research and behavioural evidence, I prioritised tracking and registration over a broad redesign.

    A tighter MVP, less unnecessary scope and a clear way to test customer value.

  3. 03 MakeAction

    Lead from research to product design — and ship faster.

    • Established an end-to-end research-to-design flow
    • Introduced native registration
    • Improved tracking
    • Strengthened accessibility
    • Built a reusable component foundation

    Insight turned into prioritised decisions, prototypes and tested journeys.

  4. 04 TransformResult

    A new way to design and deliver for a legacy brand.

    The work changed more than the product: a more integrated discovery-and-delivery model, a reusable design foundation and clearer governance for public-facing experience decisions.

    MVP in 3 months, not 12. Rating up nearly 3 stars to 4.6. Up to 90% positive sentiment.
